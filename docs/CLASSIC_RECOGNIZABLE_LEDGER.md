## Publication method and limits

The previous release held every sourced phrase for permission. That was unnecessarily broad. The US Copyright Office's [writer guidance](https://www.copyright.gov/engage/writers/) distinguishes short phrases from protected creative expression, while its [FAQ](https://www.copyright.gov/help/faq/faq-protect.html) notes that trademark protection can still apply. Its [2022 publishers study, pp. 35–36](https://www.copyright.gov/policy/publishersprotections/202206-Publishers-Protections-Study.pdf) cautions that originality matters and there is no universal safe word count.

Accordingly, the decisions below consider the expression itself, ordinary-language alternatives, creator/brand association, the proposed use, and excluded assets. They do **not** rest on attribution as permission, a rule that everything below a certain length is free, or an unqualified fair-use claim. These are implementation/editorial assessments, **not legal clearance or a worldwide rights opinion**. Launch review should reassess specific concerns if territories, branding, merchandising, advertising, or asset use changes. Known stronger branding concerns receive specific holds rather than a blanket rule for every catchphrase.

Only the short text is included. Delivery imports no third-party audio, video, music, photographs, comic artwork, characters, voices, or likenesses. It does not request accents or creator imitation and does not claim endorsement. The player is scored on the assigned direction. Metadata and this ledger explain attribution without presenting an affiliated creator pack. Existing moderation and Mature private-publication boundaries still apply.

## How wording was verified

- **Original-page visual inspection:** the actual comic panel, not alt text or a page title.
- **Original-upload captions:** the supported browser `exportYouTubeTranscript()` capability exported the transcript from the identified creator-owned upload. Exact phrase segments and timestamps were checked. Automatic versus authored/unspecified caption provenance is retained. **This is source-caption verification, not a claim that a human listened to the clip.** Full transcripts are not part of the application or committed evidence.
- **Direct interview / producer account:** quoted speech appears in the body of the original interview or first-person production account. For a later recollection or recreation, that limitation is explicit.
- **Distributed idiom:** the exact phrase is attested in a readable post or lexicographic entry. This verifies text use, not an invented first speaker. An unknown first coinage does not make an ordinary established idiom an unresolved creator quotation. Such rows are published as idioms, with the origin limitation retained.

Punctuation and sentence capitalization are editorial. Numeric punctuation in the 1987 reference follows the familiar abbreviated form; words are unchanged. Excerpts are contiguous; omitted introductory words are disclosed.

## Review and human recognition check

There is now an actual usable subset, rather than only a research backlog. It does not claim to exhaust internet culture or replace ongoing editorial judgment. The full original catalog remains the larger source of outrageous confessions, adult situations, and sharper comedy.

For playtesting, show the line before its attribution/context. Record whether the player recognizes it unaided, asks where it comes from, enjoys the performance despite not recognizing it, skips it, or chooses another interpretation. Ask recognition **after** the first take so the question does not prime an impression. Track each immutable ID and content level. Proposed evidence for keeping a reference is better completion/replay/entertainment than its matched original alternative, not a predetermined “everyone knows this” assertion. Until real participants supply that evidence, selection quality remains an editorial hypothesis.
